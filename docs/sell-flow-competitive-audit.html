Competitive Audit · Used Vehicle Acquisition

How the rest of the industry is buying cars from the public — and what we should learn from it.

A side-by-side look at the customer journey on every major sell-your-car website in May 2026 — Carvana, CarMax, Cars.com, CarGurus, AutoNation, KBB, Edmunds, Peddle, plus the dealer plug-ins (TradePending, AccuTrade, Roadster). What works, what doesn't, and what to put in front of our customers.

Prepared for: Used Car Manager
Date: May 2026
Format: Internal review

Bottom line — five things to take away from this report

  1. The market expects a real, firm offer in 2 minutes — good for 7 days. Carvana set this standard and everyone else copied it. Customers walk if you give them a "range" or a "we'll call you back."
  2. Lead with the license plate, not the VIN. Premium players (Carvana, CarMax, CarGurus, AutoNation, AccuTrade) all do this. VIN-first costs you customers who can't find the VIN on their phone in the parking lot.
  3. Two screens to a number. Plate + mileage + a few yes/no condition questions, then the offer. Anything longer bleeds customers at every step.
  4. Don't ask for an email or phone before showing the number. Every winning flow shows the offer first and collects contact info next to it. The losers gate it up front and watch their conversion rate drop.
  5. Stay on our own URL, in our own colors. Customers don't trust a redirect off the dealership website to some third-party brand. The plug-in vendors (TradePending, AccuTrade) both market this as their #1 feature for a reason.

3What each player nailed — and what they blew

One thing each company is doing right that we should steal, and one thing they're doing wrong that we should avoid.

Carvana

WinOne brand promise — "real offer in 2 minutes, good for 7 days" — repeated everywhere. Their turquoise color reads modern and tech-forward, not used-car-lot.
LossForces a "sell or trade?" choice up front, before showing any number. Customers don't know the answer yet — they just want to see what their car is worth.

CarMax

WinTwo-track design: instant online offer to grab the customer, free in-store firm offer to close them. It's the dual-track every franchise dealer should be running.
LossThe online number is hedged as a "starting point" — which contradicts their own "instant offer" pitch. Customers feel like they're being baited.

Cars.com

WinMulti-offer comparison ("private party vs. dealer vs. trade") sets honest expectations and educates the customer before they've talked to anybody.
LossBrand color palette (purples and grays) reads more like a software CMS than a car website. Doesn't match the customer's mental model of what a car-buying site looks like.

CarGurus (IMCO)

Win"Compare multiple offers in under 2 minutes" — clear, time-boxed promise. Customers know exactly what they're getting and how long it'll take.
LossThe dealer network has gaps in some states, so the promise quietly degrades — a customer in a smaller market may get fewer or no offers.

AutoNation

Win"7 days or 500 miles" makes the offer sound operational and specific, not promotional. That's a phrase to copy.
Loss5 minutes and 4–5 screens to a number — the longest in the field. Every extra screen costs them customers.

Peddle

WinYear/Make/Model dropdowns first (no plate friction for a 2002 Tahoe) — exactly right for the older / rougher tier they specialize in.
LossDense text-heavy mobile form. Looks like 2018. Functional but cheap.

KBB Instant Cash Offer

Win"Real offer for a specific amount, 7 days, redeem at a participating dealer" — the gold-standard script. Every word does work.
LossForm is long: color, engine, transmission, history, condition, contact — that's 6 sections of inputs before the customer sees a dollar amount.

Edmunds

WinShows three to four different values (trade-in, private party, dealer retail, certified pre-owned) so the customer learns what each one means.
LossThe "range" hedge confuses customers who came expecting a single number. Carvana has trained the public to expect one.

TradePending (the dominant dealer plug-in)

WinStays on the dealer's URL — no redirect, no third-party brand on screen. Best dealer-trust alignment in the field.
LossOutputs a range, not a firm number. In a Carvana-trained world, this is the wrong answer — it makes the dealer look less professional than the national online buyers.

AccuTrade (Cox Automotive's answer)

WinSingle-page collapse: plate prefills mileage and history, and the customer sees a firm VIN-level number on one screen. As frictionless as it gets.
LossRequires a mature dealer ops setup behind the scenes — most rooftops can't pull it off without retooling their reconditioning math.

4The patterns that consistently work — and the ones that consistently don't

After looking at all of the above, the same wins and losses show up over and over. This is the actionable list.

What works

  • Plate + ZIP as the first input. Resolves to a VIN behind the scenes — saves the customer 4 dropdown screens.
  • A firm offer with a 7-day hold. A range is the losing answer in 2026.
  • Two screens to a number. Plate, mileage, a few yes/no condition questions, done.
  • A visible time anchor in the headline. "2 minutes." "Instant." "90 seconds." Every winning page has one.
  • Show the offer before asking for email or phone. Email field next to the offer, not before it.
  • Stay on the dealer's URL. No redirect to a third-party brand.
  • Branching yes/no condition questions (any accidents? does it run?) — easy for the customer to answer honestly.
  • Operational language, not promotional language. "7 days or 500 miles" beats "great offer guaranteed!"

What doesn't

  • Asking for an account or email before the number. Kills conversion rate. Every top player avoids it.
  • "Excellent / Very Good / Good / Fair / Poor" condition sliders. Customers always over-rate. Use yes/no questions instead.
  • Long Year/Make/Model dropdowns when a plate would work. Looks junk-tier on a franchise dealer site.
  • Range output ($X – $Y). Carvana taught the public to expect one number. A range looks like the dealer is hedging.
  • Black hero / dark mode on a mainstream brand. Reads as buy-here-pay-here or scam. Light mode wins everywhere except true luxury.
  • Phone number above the fold on a franchise dealer site — that's a Wheelzy junk-tier signal. Push it to header utility nav.
  • Forking "sell vs. trade" before showing any value. Customer doesn't know yet — show the number, then offer the upsell.
  • FAQ accordions and testimonials above the fold. Top players strip everything to a headline and a form card. Trust signals come below.

6The recipe — what our customer should see

A concrete description of the customer's first 90 seconds on our landing page. Walk through this with marketing and we have a spec.

Above the fold (mobile, in source order)

  1. Dealer logo, top left. No third-party co-branding. The customer should feel they're on the dealership's site.
  2. Headline: "Sell your car to [Dealer Name] — real offer in 2 minutes, good for 7 days." The Carvana script, our store's name on it.
  3. A single form card: license plate field, state field (auto-filled by location, editable), one small grey link that reads "Don't have your plate? Use VIN or year/make/model."
  4. One Call-To-Action button: "Get my offer." Full-width on mobile. Primary color.
  5. One trust line directly under the CTA: "Backed by [Dealer Name] · Since [year] · [N] cars purchased this year." Add Google review stars + count if we have them.

The flow after that

  1. Screen 1: plate, state, mileage. (Plate resolves to a VIN behind the scenes — we already know the year/make/model/trim/options.)
  2. Screen 2: three to five yes/no condition questions. Title status. Any accidents? Does it run? Major mechanical? Exterior condition (one pick from three, not five — "great / decent / rough").
  3. Screen 3: the offer reveals, with a 7-day countdown badge. Email and phone fields appear next to the number, not before it. Customer can lock the offer in or schedule an appointment from this screen.

What we should NOT put above the fold

  • The dealership phone number (push it to the header utility nav)
  • A "How it works" 3-step graphic
  • A stock photo of a car the customer doesn't own
  • An inventory grid
  • A floating chat bubble on top of the form
  • A "Sell or trade?" choice (defer that until after the number)
  • An FAQ accordion or a Spanish-language toggle
  • A BBB logo (it correlates with junk-tier sellers)
